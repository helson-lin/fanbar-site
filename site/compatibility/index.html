Which Macs work with FanBar? Here is what we can prove.

FanBar supports macOS 11 and later on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs that expose readable fan data through AppleSMC. This page separates confirmed observations from missing data, so an untested model is never presented as certified.

What counts as verified

A launch screenshot alone is not enough. Maintainer verification requires an exact device, OS, FanBar build, test date, and a pass or fail result for every relevant behavior.

  1. 01App launches and remains stable
  2. 02CPU and GPU temperatures are readable
  3. 03SSD and battery temperatures are readable when the hardware exposes them
  4. 04Current, minimum, and maximum RPM are shown for every fan
  5. 05Fixed RPM changes fan output and stays inside the reported hardware range
  6. 06A temperature-based curve changes fan output as expected
  7. 07Automatic control is restored on quit, disconnect, timeout, and helper failure
  8. 08Sleep, wake, restart, and login-item behavior are stable

Compatibility, permissions, and safety

Does FanBar work on Apple Silicon?+

Yes, on Apple Silicon Macs that expose readable fan data through AppleSMC. Hardware sensors and fan behavior vary by model, so check the evidence table for the exact Mac you use. A model not listed is unverified, not necessarily unsupported.

Is FanBar free and open source?+

Yes. FanBar is free under GPL-3.0, requires no account or subscription, and publishes the complete source code, including its privileged helper.

Why does fan control need administrator permission?+

Reading temperatures does not need manual fan control. Changing fan targets talks to AppleSMC through a signed privileged helper, so macOS asks for approval before that helper can run with the required access.

What happens when FanBar quits?+

FanBar attempts to restore macOS automatic fan control when the app quits, its helper connection is lost, a timed preset ends, or the helper reports a failure. Requested targets are also clamped to each fan's reported hardware range.

Use Automatic first. Enable manual control only when you need it.
